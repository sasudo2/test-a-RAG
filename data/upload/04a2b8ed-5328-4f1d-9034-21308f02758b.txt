
**Abstract
The emergence of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) has revolutionized the analysis of non-Euclidean data, enabling deep learning to be applied to complex structures such as social networks, citation graphs, and molecular systems. This review paper traces the architectural evolution of GNNs, focusing on the pivotal transition from early **recursive state-based models** to the modern **Neural Message Passing** paradigm. We begin by examining the foundational work of **Scarselli et al. (2009)** and **Micheli (2009)**, who established the first formal frameworks using contraction maps and constructive feedforward approaches to achieve node equilibrium.

We then analyze the "Spectral Shift," where researchers like **Bruna et al. (2014)** and **Kipf & Welling (2017)** simplified complex graph signal processing into efficient convolutional layers. The core of this review centers on the unification of these diverse methods under the **Message Passing Neural Network (MPNN)** framework proposed by **Gilmer et al. (2017)**. By framing GNNs as a two-phase process of message aggregation and state updating, the MPNN paradigm provided a universal language that encompasses modern variants such as **GraphSAGE (Hamilton et al., 2017)** and **Graph Attention Networks**. Through this historical lens, we highlight how the field moved from computationally intensive recursive iterations to scalable, layer-wise abstractions, ultimately enabling the high-performance graph representation learning used in quantum chemistry and large-scale web applications today.

Keywords: Graph Neural Networks, Message Passing, Recursive Neural Networks, Graph Convolutional Networks, Representation Learning, MPNN.
